# Hook bank — 30 verbatim openings, sorted by what they do

Thirty openings, hand-sorted into eight families, drawn from the 53-short transcript corpus
(the other 23 are duplicates of these shapes). Every line below is a **verbatim first
sentence** from a @kallaway.marketing short, with its play count. Use these as slot-machines: keep the skeleton, swap the subject. Do not copy the
words — copy the *shape*.

---

## 1. NEGATIVE DIAGNOSIS — "the reason your X is failing is Y"
The single highest-conversion family for teaching content. It names the viewer's problem
before offering the fix, so the viewer self-selects in the first three words.

| Plays | Line |
|---|---|
| 20.5K | "The reason your videos don't hold attention is because you're using **the wrong story arc**." |
| 4.8K | "The reason your videos are not working is because you're **focused on the wrong thing**." |
| 3.9K | "The main reason your personal brand is failing is that you haven't built **enough trust at scale**." |
| 3.4K | "The reason your content doesn't make you money is because you're making **the wrong types of videos**." |

**Skeleton:** `The reason your [OUTCOME THEY WANT] isn't happening is because you're [SPECIFIC WRONG THING].`
The wrong thing must be *nameable* — "the wrong story arc", not "bad content".

# The naming law: NAME THE THING

In nearly every video, the sentence right after the hook lands gives the framework a proprietary
name. This is the highest-leverage move in the entire method and costs nothing.

> **Where exactly?** **Immediately after the snapback closes, always before the first body
> point.** That's sentence two after a one-line hook and sentence four after the full three-step
> hook — it depends on how you punctuate, so *find the snapback and put the name next* rather
> than counting to two. → SKILL.md "Reconciling the rules" §1.

> "This is called **the triple hook method**."
> "It's called **the double tap**."
> "It's called **the dopamine ladder**."
> "It's called **embedded truths**."
> "It's called **Bullseye positioning**."
> "It's called **the empty calorie content trap**."
> "I call these **the Four Horsemen of winning content**."
> "This is called **the 6 by 5 method**."
> "These are called **S-tier ranking videos**."

A named thing is quotable, searchable, teachable, and feels like IP you can't get elsewhere.
An unnamed tip is a tip. **Never ship a framework without a name.**

# The CTA machine (51 of 53 shorts, median position 96% through)

> ⚠️ **This is the media-game CTA.** It's correct for Absurdity / REKT / BLACK BOX / THE MACHINE.
> On any video selling Kiwuuu, use the **offer-led CTA** instead — the keyword version
> manufactures leads into a close that converts at zero.
> → [../references/ops.md §8b](../references/ops.md)

## The formula
```
[NAME THE ASSET WITH A NUMBER IN IT]
+ [ONE LINE ON WHY IT'S VALUABLE / WHO ELSE HAS IT]
+ "comment [ONE-WORD KEYWORD] and I'll send [it / you the whole thing] [for free]"
```

## Verbatim examples
> "I made this database with the **top 47 visual hooks** and a ton of examples for each one.
> Comment **hooks** and I'll send you the whole thing."

> "It's kind of like a cheat sheet that you can just feed to AI so you never write a flat
> script again. Comment **story**, and I'll send through the whole thing for free."

> "**Over 700,000 people have watched this** and I'm telling you it's probably the best hook
> training on the internet. Comment **hooks** and I'll send it to you so you can watch it."

> "Think of this like your **content team in a box**. Comment **team** and I'll send you the
> entire handbook for free as well as all the links to the exact people I use."

## Rules the corpus proves
1. **One keyword per framework**, not per channel. He runs 29 distinct keywords —
   `hooks`, `story`, `dopamine`, `ladder`, `bullseye`, `formats`, `team`, `trust`,
   `horseman`, `double`, `plugin`, `bridge`, `system`, `park`, `creator`, `collections`, `AI`, `MCP`, `Claude`…
2. **The asset always has a count**: 47 visual hooks, 20 formats, 18 pages, 14 skills,
   31 layouts, 10 examples. A number makes the promise finite and therefore believable.
3. **It goes last**, after the payload is fully delivered — never mid-roll.
4. **"For free" appears explicitly** in most of them. Say the price even when it's zero.
5. The comment keyword doubles as the algorithm play — comments are the cheapest
   engagement signal to manufacture, and he has a whole video on exactly that.

## The loop-back closer (rare, but the sharpest thing in the corpus)
> "Oh, and by the way, **when you let this video loop, you'll see how I use the word 'but'
> to hook you in the beginning**."

The last line teaches the viewer to rewatch the first line. In short form the video loops by
default — this converts a scroll-away into a second view and a demonstration of the lesson at
the same time.
