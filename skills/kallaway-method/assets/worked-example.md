# Worked example — one 60s Kiwuuu short, built in order

The whole method applied end to end on a real Kiwuuu situation, so you can see how the pieces
sit together rather than inferring it from six reference files.

> **This is a demonstration artifact, not approved copy.** Kiwu approves scripts; this skill
> doesn't.
> **Every number below was counted, not estimated** — see "Verifying this file" at the bottom.
> If you edit the script, re-run the counter. A worked example that misreports itself teaches
> the error with the authority of a demonstration.

**Brief:** Kiwuuu sells WhatsApp AI agents to business owners. Real situation: 287 leads, 113
replies, 0 conversions. The product answers customer WhatsApp messages automatically.

---

## Step 0 — Idea gate

| Check | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Uncommon idea, or common + unique lens? | **Common + unique lens.** "AI answers your messages" is common; "the fast reply is what kills the sale" is the lens |
| Large enough audience? | Yes — every business owner with an inbox |
| Angle: shocking or useful? | **Useful** (education) |
| Inside our one topic? | Yes |
| Share test — would sharing make them look in-the-know? | Yes: it reframes something they thought was solved |
| Commoditisation test — could 50 people make this? | **Only survivable because of the real 287/113/0 number.** That is the one-of-one |

## Step 1 — Packaging (first, before any script)

- **Desire loop** — *pain:* leads reply then vanish · *solution:* replies that open loops
  instead of closing them · *outcome:* conversations that reach a booking.
- **In-video title text** (on screen at 0:00, big, **never spoken**):
  > **YOUR AI REPLIES TOO WELL**
  Shares no phrase with the spoken opening — "replies too well" vs "replies in ten seconds".
  Same *meaning* (alignment ✓), different *words* (no duplication ✓).
- **Grid cover** (browsed later on the profile, for bingeability): same phone still + the words
  "the reply problem" — so a visitor scanning the grid knows what this one is.
- **Second-one visual:** a phone screen, WhatsApp thread mid-scroll, thumb flicking fast up a
  wall of green outbound bubbles all answered in seconds — the motion *is* the scroll — then it
  stops dead on the last message with no reply beneath it. Hard cut on "But."
- Two elements only. Nothing bottom-right.

## Step 2 — Outline (the hard gate)

| Rank | Point | What / Why / How | Non-obvious? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | A perfect answer ends the conversation | *What:* the AI resolves the question · *Why:* a closed loop has no next step · *How:* every reply ends with a question | ✅ **yes — this is the head fake** |
| **2** | A reply is not a buying signal | *What:* replies measure curiosity · *Why:* engagement ≠ intent · *How:* score replies, don't count them | partly |
| **3** | Message two never gets sent | *What:* fast first reply, then silence · *Why:* nobody owns follow-up · *How:* the agent owns message two | ✅ yes |

**Gate passed** — point 1 is genuinely non-obvious.
**Runtime 60s → 3 points → order 2-1-3** (the 2-1-3-4 pattern truncated, not padded).
**Native embed:** none separately — in a 60-second offer-led short the CTA *is* the embed. Don't
claim an embed you aren't going to write.

## Step 3 — The script

> **[0:00 · title text on screen: YOUR AI REPLIES TOO WELL]**
>
> **[context lean]** "Your WhatsApp AI replies in ten seconds, and you're told that's what
> closes sales.
>
> **[scroll-stop interjection — ONE sentence, deliberately]** **But** I ran that on 287 business
> owners — 113 replied, zero bought.
>
> **[contrarian snapback]** The reply is where the sale dies, not where it starts.
>
> **[name it — sentence 4]** It's called the Second Message Gap.
>
> **[loop opener]** *But here's the thing about a reply:*
> **[point 2]** it measures curiosity, not money. Asking your price isn't a pipeline, it's noise
> with a green tick.
>
> **[loop opener]** *And this is where it actually breaks.*
> **[point 1 — the head fake]** Your AI answers perfectly, and ends the conversation. A closed
> loop has no next step, so every reply has to end with a question.
>
> **[loop opener]** *Most owners never get to the part that pays.*
> **[point 3]** Message two. Your agent replies in four seconds, then goes silent for four days.
> Message two is the sale, and nobody owns it.
>
> **[outro]** You don't have a lead problem. You have a Second Message Gap, and the leads you
> paid for are sitting in it.
>
> **[CTA — offer-led, not keyword]** Under 50 messages a day? Ignore this. Past 200 and losing
> the late-night ones? Tell me your after-hours volume and I'll tell you what it's costing you."

**180 words · 62s at 2.9 w/s · 19 sentences · framework name at sentence 4 · zero hedges.**
*(All five counted by the script at the bottom of this file, not estimated.)*

|---|---|
| 0 Idea | PASS 6/6 |

## What this example is demonstrating

1. **Packaging really comes first** — the title text was written before a word of script and it
   shaped the opening visual.
2. **The interjection is deliberately ONE sentence.** That is what puts the framework name at
   sentence 4. Write the interjection as three short sentences and the name slides to six — the
   rule is *"name it before the first body point"*, and the sentence count follows from how you
   punctuate the hook.
3. **"I" appears twice and "me" once — all three carry proof or the offer**, never the pain.
   Every pain statement is "you" or "your".
4. **Three loop openers for three points** — the ratio rule, not a stopwatch.
5. **The CTA is offer-led**, because this is a Kiwuuu video. The keyword version ("comment GAP
   and I'll send the nine templates") would pass every other gate and still be wrong: it
   manufactures leads into a close that converts at zero.
6. **The CTA uses question-form disqualifiers, not "if" clauses** — *"Under 50 messages a day?"*
   rather than *"If you're doing under 50…"*. That satisfies the disqualifier requirement and
   the hedge sweep at the same time. See SKILL.md "Reconciling the rules" §5.
7. **The head fake sits in slot two**, where 2-1-3 puts it — a good point, then a better one, so
   value is visibly rising.

## Verifying this file

Any edit to the script invalidates the numbers above. **This script is the live copy — run it,
don't estimate.** It reproduces every claim in the results line. Edit the `S` list to check your
own script.

```python
import re
S = [
 "Your WhatsApp AI replies in ten seconds, and you're told that's what closes sales.",
 "But I ran that on 287 business owners — 113 replied, zero bought.",
 "The reply is where the sale dies, not where it starts.",
 "It's called the Second Message Gap.",
 "But here's the thing about a reply: it measures curiosity, not money. Asking your price isn't a pipeline, it's noise with a green tick.",
 "And this is where it actually breaks.",
 "Your AI answers perfectly, and ends the conversation. A closed loop has no next step, so every reply has to end with a question.",
 "Most owners never get to the part that pays.",
 "Message two. Your agent replies in four seconds, then goes silent for four days. Message two is the sale, and nobody owns it.",
 "You don't have a lead problem. You have a Second Message Gap, and the leads you paid for are sitting in it.",
 "Under 50 messages a day? Ignore this. Past 200 and losing the late-night ones? Tell me your after-hours volume and I'll tell you what it's costing you.",
]
NAME = "called the Second Message Gap"       # your framework name
full  = " ".join(S)
w     = len(full.split())
sents = [s for t in S for s in re.split(r'(?<=[.!?])\s+', t) if s.strip()]
print(f"words {w} -> {w/2.9:.1f}s | sentences {len(sents)} | "
      f"name at #{next(i for i,s in enumerate(sents,1) if NAME in s)}")
print("hedges:", [h for h in ['if ','maybe','might','could','probably'] if h in full.lower()] or "clean")
print("first-person:", re.findall(r"\b(I|I'll|me|my)\b", full))
```

Expected output for the script above — if you get anything else, the file has drifted:

```
words 180 -> 62.1s | sentences 19 | name at #4
hedges: clean
first-person: ['I', 'me', 'I']
```

**Note on the sentence count:** the colon in *"But here's the thing about a reply: it
measures…"* is **not** a sentence break under this regex, which is why the loop opener and
point 2 count as one sentence. Nineteen, not twenty.
