## The five things that are always true

Everything else in this skill is elaboration on these.

1. **Share rate is the only metric.** `shares ÷ views`. Above **3%** and sustained, it goes
   viral. Every other technique here exists to raise that one number. People share to earn
   social credit — the test is *"would my viewer look smart, helpful, or in-the-know to their
   peers if they shared this?"*
2. **A hook only has two jobs**: topic clarity (they know instantly what this is) and
   on-target curiosity (they believe it's for them and want the next line). If a hook fails,
   it failed at exactly one of four things — delay, confusion, irrelevance, disinterest.
3. **The climax is not in the middle.** Open at 70/100 intensity, spike to 90 within 5–10s,
   release, and re-peak roughly every 10 seconds until the end. Attention is re-earned
   continuously, never front-loaded.
4. **Name the thing.** Nearly every one of his videos gives the idea a proprietary name in the
   sentence right after the hook lands — "the triple hook method", "the double tap", "the Four
   Horsemen". A named framework is quotable, teachable, and feels like IP. An unnamed tip is a
   tip. *(Exact placement is positional, not a sentence index — see "Reconciling the rules" §1.)*
5. **Contrast is the engine.** Every hook, every re-hook, every body point is the distance
   between *what the viewer currently believes* and *what you're about to tell them*. No
   contrast, no curiosity, no retention. Ask on every beat: **what do most people believe that
   I disagree with?**

## Reconciling the rules — read this before you hit a contradiction

Seven pairs of rules in this skill look like they collide. They don't, but only if you know
which one governs. Found by cold-testing the skill; resolved here so you don't adjudicate alone.

**1. "Name it in sentence two" vs the three-sentence hook.**
**The rule is positional, not an index: name it in the sentence immediately after the snapback
closes, and always before the first body point.** Where that falls depends entirely on how you
punctuate the hook — sentence two after a one-line compressed hook, sentence four after a
three-sentence hook, sentence six if you wrote the interjection as three short sentences. Both
of the first two appear in his corpus. **Never count sentences to find the slot; find the
snapback and put it next.**

**2. "Say you, not I" vs "prove it with your own real failure".**
Different jobs. **The pain is always theirs; the proof may be yours.**
- ❌ `"I've struggled with X my whole life"` — pain in first person makes them audit whether
  they're like you.
- ✅ `"If you've struggled with X…"` — pain in second person.
- ✅ `"I tested this on 287 businesses and 0 converted"` — proof in first person is *required*
  when your one-of-one credibility is the reason the video isn't commoditised.

**3. "Order 2-1-3-4" vs "three loops per 60 seconds".** Point count scales with runtime; the
2-1-3-4 *ordering* holds at any count. Truncate the pattern, don't pad the script:

| Runtime | Words | Body points | Order |
|---|---|---|---|
| 30s | ~85 | 2 | 2-1 |
| **60s** | **~175** | **3** | **2-1-3** |
| 90s | ~260 | 3–4 | 2-1-3(-4) |
| 2min+ | ~175/min | 4+ | 2-1-3-4… |

*(~2.9 words/second at short-form pace. His own shorts run 49–147s, median ~93s.)*

**4. The comment-keyword CTA vs the Kiwuuu warning.** The keyword CTA is correct for a
media-game account and **wrong for ours** — it manufactures leads into a close that already
converts at zero. Use the offer-led CTA instead: [references/ops.md §8b](references/ops.md).
*(`hook-bank.md`, `script.md` and the core loop all show the keyword formula because it's what
his corpus does — it is the right pattern for Absurdity/REKT/BLACK BOX/THE MACHINE. It is only
wrong on Kiwuuu-selling videos.)*

**5. "Delete every `if`" vs the conditional-filter hooks and the CTA disqualifier.**
Two different `if`s. **The hedge sweep only kills the first:**

| `if` type | What it modifies | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **Hedge** | *your claim* — "this **might** work **if** you try it" | ❌ Delete. Swap to `when`. Every one is an exit door |
| **Filter** | *the viewer* — "**if** you've struggled with acne", "**if** you're under 50 messages a day" | ✅ **Keep.** This is a qualifier, not uncertainty. It's how the Conditional Filter hook family works, and how the CTA disqualifier works |

So `hook-bank.md` §7 (`If you want [DESIRE], [INSTRUCTION]`) and `hooks.md`'s
*"If you've struggled with skin problems…"* are **correct as written** and survive the sweep.

**Style preference, not a rule:** filters read punchier as questions —
*"Under 50 messages a day? Ignore this."* beats *"If you're doing under 50 messages a day,
don't bother."* Use the question form in CTAs where the disqualifier needs to land hard; keep
`if` in hooks where the conditional is doing gentle self-selection.

**6. The 3-step hook vs the 5-part intro framework.** Same structure at two scales, not two
competing systems. The 5-part intro (`script.md` step 3) is the **long-form** version and
contains the 3-step inside it:

| 3-step (short form, 0–5s) | 5-part intro (long form, 0–30s) |
|---|---|
| Context lean | 1. Immediate context + 2. State the common belief |
| Scroll-stop interjection | *(the pivot into 3)* |
| Contrarian snapback | 3. Contrarian take |
| — | 4. Proof · 5. Plan *(long-form only — there's runway for them)* |

**In a short, stop after the snapback and name the thing.** Proof and plan are what you add when
you have 30 seconds of intro instead of 5.

**7. "Alignment" vs "the cover must not repeat the title".**
Two different words: **align on meaning, differ on wording.** All three surfaces must point at
the same idea so comprehension clicks — but if the on-screen text is a transcript of your spoken
line, you've spent a surface to say something twice. Title text should carry the *angle* the
spoken line doesn't. Spoken *"your AI replies in ten seconds"* + text **"YOUR AI REPLIES TOO
WELL"** — same idea, no shared phrase, two surfaces doing two jobs.

## The core loop — run this for any piece of content

```
IDEA      → Is it uncommon, or common-with-a-unique-lens? Does it have a large enough
             audience to travel? (Never uncommon idea + uncommon lens — too niche to move.)
ANGLE     → Shocking (entertainment) or Useful (education). There is no third option.
PACKAGE   → Title/cover/thumbnail FIRST, before the script. If the packaging can't win,
             the script is wasted work.
OUTLINE   → Bullet the body points. Gut-check for uniqueness. HARD GATE: if nothing here
             is genuinely non-obvious, do not write the script. Go research more.
HOOK      → Context Lean → Scroll-Stop Interjection → Contrarian Snapback. Then screen
             against the 4 commandments (alignment, speed-to-value, clarity, curiosity).
BODY      → Order points 2-1-3-4 (second-best first, best second). Re-hook between every
             point by opening new stakes as you close the old loop.
OUTRO     → Close the loop, leave on a high note, then the CTA.
CTA       → Placed LAST, never mid-roll. Which one depends on the account:
              media-game channel  → asset with a number + one comment keyword
              anything selling Kiwuuu → offer-led (disqualifier + the one buyer number)
              → references/ops.md §8b. Getting this wrong is a preflight FAIL.
```

## The Seven Lego Bricks — the operating system

His core mental model, and the fastest way to make something that works. Any short-form video
decomposes into seven atomic parts:

1. **Topic** — what it's about *(changes every video)*
2. **Angle** — the take on it *(changes every video)*
3. **Hook structure** — visual + spoken + text hook
4. **Story structure** — the video type (breakdown, listicle, case study, narrative…)
5. **Visual format** — split screen, full screen, POV, talking head, screencast…
6. **Key visuals** — A-roll, B-roll, graphics
7. **Audio** — music and sound effects

**The procedure:** find what's already winning in your category, explode those videos into the
seven bricks, **hold constant the bricks that are already world-class, change only the ones
that aren't**, restack, ship. For anyone not yet expert, only bricks 1 and 2 should change per
video. Inventing new visual formats is an advanced move — beginners fail because they try to
invent when they should be recombining.

> "I don't really think you should copy — but *oh, I see the split screen with the motion from
> left to right is catching my eye, I'm going to do that for my visual execution, but I'm going
> to switch the topic.*"

## Where he is wrong for us — read before applying

He is a creator-economy operator selling to creators. Kiwuuu sells WhatsApp AI agents to
business owners. Three of his defaults do not transfer:

1. **His whole funnel is the comment-keyword lead magnet** (51 of 53 shorts). It works because
   he has a library of databases to give away. We have **287 leads and 0 conversions** — our
   constraint is the sales conversation, not lead volume. Use his *structure* freely; do not
   assume his *CTA* fixes our problem. More leads into a broken close is more waste.
2. **He plays the media game and says so.** By his own Five Content Games framework, Kiwuuu is
   bucket five — B2B, education-heavy, personal brand accruing trust to sell something. His
   view: *"you will make way more money playing the products/services/offers game than the
   media game."* Optimize for the right buyer, not the biggest number.
3. **Sandcastles.ai — his product — duplicates `kiwuuu-analyze`.** Outlier search, transcript,
   hook/structure analysis. Do not buy it to do what's already wired. The genuinely
   non-duplicated parts are the outlier-score database and competitor watchlists.

Everything else — the hook stack, the arc, the re-hook cadence, the Lego bricks, the packaging
psychology — is format-agnostic and transfers cleanly.
