## 1. Share rate is the metric

```
SHARE RATE = TOTAL SHARES ÷ TOTAL VIEWS
```

> **Above 3% and sustained, it goes viral.** For every 100 people who watch, three must send it
> to a friend. "That is literally all that matters in content."

**The psychology behind it:** people share to earn credibility or social status — if it makes
them laugh, look smart, or earn respect. **It's a currency.** If you're the fourth person
sharing the same common story, there's no social credit left to earn.

**The pre-publish gate — use verbatim:**
> *"Would my target viewer look smart, helpful, or in-the-know to their peers if they shared
> this?"*

Secondary **text test:** would you immediately text this to a friend who has this problem?

**Four requirements for shareworthy content:** feel novel · provide on-target value · be
concise · have contrast.

## 2. How the algorithm actually works — the sample-pool cascade

1. **You post.** The platform transcribes the video in real time and builds a topic map. **The
   transcript is the main signal** — "the video is basically a long-tail blog post." Hashtags
   and keywords are "icing on the cake"; the transcription is the cake. → *Say your keywords out
   loud in the script.*
2. **It builds a test sample pool** — say 200 people, mixed: some existing followers, some new.
3. **It blasts to those 200.** On YouTube long-form, whether they click the thumbnail matters.
   On Shorts it doesn't.
4. **It compares:** did that group watch and engage **more than usual, the same, or less**?
5. **More than usual** → next sample is an order of magnitude larger (200 → 2,000) with a
   similar makeup. **Neutral** → maybe another 200. **Bad** → throttled fast. *That's why a bad
   video dies at 200 views.*
6. **Concentric circles.** Each ring gets muddier. His 2M-view storytelling video: ring 1 = his
   followers, ring 2 = people who like storytelling, ring 3 = scriptwriters and
   cinematographers — "it started to get muddy." Eventually it runs out of people and throttles.

**Two operational consequences:**
- Your existing followers must be **hyper-targeted on your topic**, so the first sample comes
  back hot.
- **Topical consistency video to video** — so the same type of person likes it repeatedly and
  the algorithm has a stable audience profile to build rings from.

This is why "post everything on one brand channel" no longer works. His highest-viewed short
(198.9K) argues exactly this: **stop running a variety show, build a network of channels**, each
locked to one topic, format and audience goal.

## 3. Retention — his actual position

1. **He doesn't look at retention charts at all.** Pure intuition, video by video.
2. **His audit method:** watch it one sentence at a time and ask *"am I clear on what this
   sentence is and what I should take away from it?"* Where it isn't clear, that's the drop-off.
3. **The one number if you don't have that muscle yet:** on YouTube Shorts, you want **~70%
   retention after the first 3 seconds**. Above 70% = banger. Below 70% = problem. **Below 50% =
   huge problem.**
4. **Why he discounts it:** data is confirmatory, a lagging indicator. Seeing 49% only tells you
   to make the hook better — which you should have done anyway. The leading indicator is
   following the principles while making the video.
5. **The trap:** people get obsessed with data and it distracts from the actual job. "If it
   isn't clear to you after 10 watches, it isn't clear to them."

## 4. Content minutes — the trust ledger

> "To get someone to trust you and ultimately buy, they need to watch a certain number of
> minutes of your content." Each minute watched = one **block**.

**Block cost by conversion event (his numbers):**

| Action | Blocks |
|---|---|
| Follow | 3–5 |
| Comment | 7–10 |
| Email opt-in | ~4 |
| Low-ticket purchase (~$97) | 8–20 |
| High-ticket purchase (~$10K) | **400+** |

⚠️ **Only watched minutes count. Impressions accrue nothing.**

**Consequence 1 — bingeability.** When someone discovers you, you want them to stack blocks in
one session. Two levers: **numbered series**, and **cover design that makes the profile grid
browsable**.

**Consequence 2 — long form banks trust far faster.** A 25-minute video with 8–10 min average
view duration banks ~10 blocks per rep. A 60-second short with 30s average watch banks half a
block. **It takes ~20 shorts to equal one long-form video in trust accrual.** Plus long form is
intent-based — they chose it rather than being fed it.

**Trust adhesion — what makes minutes stick.** Content must be simultaneously **non-obvious**
and **tactically useful**. Three diagnostic questions:
- What do you believe that 99% of your niche hasn't heard?
- What would most people in your niche disagree with?
- What did hands-on experience teach you that nobody talks about?

## 5. Comments — the cheapest lever

Comments are the easiest engagement signal to manufacture, which is why his CTA is a comment
keyword 51 times out of 53. Five ways he names to drive them without manufacturing controversy:
ask a direct question in the video · leave a deliberate small gap the audience will correct ·
comment-to-receive a resource · reply to every early comment to boost the thread · pin a comment
that continues the video's argument.

> ⚠️ **This is the media-game lever.** It buys algorithmic distribution, which is the right goal
> on Absurdity / REKT / BLACK BOX / THE MACHINE. On a video selling Kiwuuu it optimises the one
> stage that isn't broken — see **§8b**, where the metric becomes *qualified replies*, not
> comment volume. The two sections are not in conflict; they serve different accounts.

## 6. The production loop and where the leverage is

```
IDEA → RESEARCH → SCRIPTING → RECORDING → EDITING → POSTING
```

| Stage | Leverage |
|---|---|
| **Editing** | Near-commoditised. Hire it out early — solved quickly |
| **Recording** | Must be him. An AI avatar "doesn't save that much time" |
| **Idea + research + scripting** | **Where all the real time-saving sauce is** — and the hardest to automate |

His current reality: pull an idea off the operator's short list → **write the script in 20
minutes** → shoot → hand to editor → comes back done.

## 7. The content operator — cloning the idea function

> "As an entrepreneur, you're just in the cloning business."

The role he hired is a **content operator** (titled "EA Plus"), overseas. What he cloned in:
what makes a good idea, how to look for ideas, how to filter good vs *great*, how to develop
them. Codified into a **180-page Google Doc** plus live calls (which work best) and Looms.

**The upskilling shortcut:** the operator watched every video on his channel plus the whole
curriculum over **two weeks**, taking notes — and came back using Kane's terminology by default.
Claim: any raw hire could come out **90% upskilled** with no direct effort.

**The daily system — idea pipes.** The operator scans defined sources. His pipes for tech/AI:
other creators in the niche, Twitter, Reddit, YouTube, newsletters. *Different niches have
different pipes.*

**Developing an idea** means: build a short list, then for each one find the **visuals** (from
the company, from Twitter, live examples), and specify what's interesting, what the **angle**
could be, and what the visuals are.

**The artifact:** one Google Doc, **one tab per idea**.

**The pitch call**, once or twice a week, "like a showrunner would in Hollywood." Each pitch
contains: the idea · what he found · **time sensitivity** ("if we make it, it has to be in 24
hours") · why it's good · what we could do with it · **and the cons — why we wouldn't make it.**

**Timeline:** 3–6 months to clone yourself this way. "The strategy is the easy part; the
iterative looping is the hard part" — you have to run hundreds of ideas yes/no/yes/no before
they learn what you actually think.

**His team shape:** 1 long-form YouTube editor (4–5 videos/mo) · a packaging team for titles and
thumbnails · 1 short-form editor (16–20 shorts/mo) · management for brand deals. He hired from
day one.

## 8. Lead magnets — the five types

> "All a lead magnet is, is getting someone's email in exchange for free value."

1. **One-off PDF or doc** — the easiest. *"I went through that fast; if you want it written
   down, link below."*
2. **3- or 5-day free email series** — for when a doc would be too dense.
3. **Free community**, email captured on the way in.
4. **Landing page / waitlist.**
5. **A form** — "if you want our editors, fill out the form."

**The machine:** content → lead magnet → email → drip (automated sequence or weekly send) →
ask for the sale. Email because open and trust rates stay high and "it's the only place not
fully taken over by algorithms — you still control your own inbox." His stated open rate: 60%.

> ⚠️ **Kiwuuu caveat.** This is his entire funnel, and it's built for someone with a library of
> giveaway assets and a low-ticket offer. Our problem is **287 leads / 113 replies / 0
> conversions** — a sales problem, not a lead-volume problem. Copy the *structure* of his CTA
> if it fits a campaign; do not assume more leads fixes our number. See [[kiwuuu-pricing]].

## 8b. The offer-led CTA — our replacement for the keyword machine

**Not his doctrine. Ours, derived from his own frameworks**, because the keyword CTA is the one
place his system actively works against us. Use this whenever the video is for Kiwuuu.

### Why the keyword CTA fails here — using his own numbers

His **content-minutes** ledger says an email opt-in costs ~4 watched minutes and a **high-ticket
purchase costs 400+**. A comment keyword harvests people at the 1-minute mark and drops them into
a nurture sequence built for a $97 product. A WhatsApp-agent build is not a $97 product. So the
keyword CTA is optimising the *one* stage that isn't broken: **113 of 287 already replied.** They
engage. They don't buy. Adding a 288th lead changes nothing.

His **Views Trap** names the failure precisely: broad audience → build the offer → not enough of
them actually want it. And his **trust adhesion** test names the other half: trust can accrue to
the wrong domain — they may trust us as *someone who knows AI* rather than *the person who fixes
their specific revenue leak*.

### The three moves that replace it

**1. QUALIFY DOWN, NOT UP.** The keyword CTA maximises responders. Ours should **deliberately
shrink** the pool to people who have the problem badly enough to pay. Name the disqualifier out
loud — it raises trust and it filters:
> ❌ "Comment AGENT and I'll send the setup guide."
> ✅ "Under 50 customer messages a day? Ignore this — you'll answer them faster yourself. Past
> 200 a day and losing the late-night ones? That's the whole problem this solves."

*Write the disqualifier as a **question**, not an `if` clause. Same filter, lands harder, and it
keeps the hedge sweep clean — see SKILL.md "Reconciling the rules" §5.*

**2. ASK FOR THE DIAGNOSIS, NOT THE ASSET.** The asset CTA gets an email. The diagnosis CTA gets
a **qualified conversation** — and it's the one CTA where their reply carries the information
that decides whether they're a buyer.
> ✅ "Tell me your after-hours message volume and I'll tell you what it's costing you.
> Costing you nothing? I'll say so."

The offer must be **a specific number about their business**, given free, in public.

**3. SEND THEM TO DEPTH, NOT TO A FORM.** If they need 400 blocks of trust and they've watched
one, the correct next step is *more watching*, not a purchase ask. Point at the longest, most
specific asset we have — a real deployment with real numbers.
> ✅ "There's a full teardown of one of these builds — what it cost, what broke, what it
> actually returned. That's the one to watch before you talk to anyone about this."

### The pattern

```
[NAME THE DISQUALIFIER — who this is NOT for]
+ [THE ONE NUMBER that identifies a real buyer]
+ [ask for that number, or point at the deepest asset]
```

**Never** a keyword for a generic asset. **Never** a volume ask.

### The metric that goes with it

Stop counting comments. Count **qualified replies** — a reply containing a real number about
their business. On a Kiwuuu video, **five qualified replies beats 300 comments**, because our
constraint is at the close, not the top.

> ⚠️ **Before optimising any content for reach, check whether reach is the binding constraint.**
> For Kiwuuu it currently isn't. If a video plan's success metric is views or leads, it is
> probably solving the wrong problem — say so plainly, then make the video anyway if asked.

| Job | Tool | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Research | **ChatGPT o3 + Deep Research** | "The king combo." Full report in 5–10 min |
| Creative writing | **Claude Projects** | Better at writing than ChatGPT; ChatGPT better at research |
| Outlier search / analysis | **Sandcastles.ai** (his own product) | ⚠️ duplicates `kiwuuu-analyze` — see below |
| Profile re-sorting | **SortFeed** | Chrome extension, re-sorts any IG/TikTok profile by views |
| Images | **Midjourney** (aesthetics), **ChatGPT 4o image** (when text must render cleanly) | |
| Image + video, one front end | **Krea.ai** | Hot-swap model dropdown; shared team workspaces |
| Video gen | **Veo 3 / Flow** (audio baked in), Kling 2.0, WAN 2.1, Runway | |
| Voice / SFX | **ElevenLabs** | Voice clone from 30 min–3 hrs of clean audio |
| Avatar | **HeyGen** + ElevenLabs voice via API | Short form only; fails for long-form face content |
| Dictation | **Wispr Flow / Superwhisper** | "Talking is 4× faster than typing" |
| Thumbnail research | **1of10, VidIQ** | Also for saving a swipe file |
| Thumbnail testing | **ClickPilot.app, ThumbnailTest.com**, YouTube native ABC test | |
| Upscaling | **Magnific.ai** | |

**Two prompt patterns worth stealing:**

1. **End every big prompt with an invitation to ask questions first.** He calls this his single
   biggest AI tip — when it asked him to link competitor channels and he added 4–5, results
   improved dramatically.
   > "Feel free to ask me any follow-up questions before you run the full process."

2. **B-roll sourcing prompt** — give the model your finished script and ask for 10 YouTube
   source links with the usable clip spot in each, mapped line by line. Saves an editor 30–60
   minutes per video.

> ⚠️ **Sandcastles.ai duplicates `kiwuuu-analyze`.** Outlier search, transcripts, hook/structure
> analysis — all already wired here. Per the 2026-08 tool review, 5 of 7 researched tools
> duplicated something we already had. The genuinely non-duplicated parts are the outlier-score
> *database* and competitor watchlists. Don't buy to replace what's built.

## 10. Where the window is closing

**On the media game:** *"That game's going to zero — AI content will run it over. The media game
is a dumb game to start in 2026."*

**On distribution arbitrage:** the current cheap-organic-reach window is closing as supply rises
and AI content floods every transactional format. The durable move is toward one-of-one
formats — lived experience, narrative, and things that are genuinely hard to copy.

**Implication for Kiwuuu:** don't compete on volume of generic explainers. Compete on the thing
nobody else has — real deployments, real numbers, real failures from actually shipping WhatsApp
agents.
