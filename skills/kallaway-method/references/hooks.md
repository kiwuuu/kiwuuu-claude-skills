# Hooks — the first five seconds

A hook has exactly **two jobs**: give **topic clarity** (the viewer instantly knows what this
is) and **on-target curiosity** (they believe it's for them and need the next line). Do both
and the viewer opts in. Everything below serves those two outputs.

---

## 1. The hook is three surfaces, not one

| Surface | What it is | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Visual hook** | What's on screen in second one | Processed first — light beats sound |
| **Text hook** | Big **title text**, not captions | Read faster than speech is heard |
| **Spoken hook** | The first line you say | Ranks *third* in importance |

**Alignment is the whole game.** All three must point at the **same idea**. If the visual shows
one thing while you say another, comprehension drops and the viewer churns. He calls the gap
between a 5K video and a 500K video "hook alignment" more often than hook quality.

> His example: show a robot with visible tendons + say *"this is the first robot ever to have
> tendons like a human"* + title text *"robot has human tendons."* The viewer's assumption gets
> confirmed, which "clicks" in the brain, and they lock in.

⚠️ **Align on meaning, differ on wording.** His example above is deliberately literal to teach
the concept, but title text that transcribes your spoken line burns a surface saying one thing
twice. The stronger version carries the *angle* the spoken line doesn't: spoken *"your AI replies
in ten seconds"* + text **"YOUR AI REPLIES TOO WELL"** — same idea, no shared phrase, two
surfaces doing two jobs. → SKILL.md "Reconciling the rules" §7.

**Two kinds of on-screen text — don't confuse them:**
- **Captions** — the spoken words, oscillating word by word.
- **Title text** — big words at top or bottom that you do *not* say aloud. This is where the
  context and the curiosity gap live. `"Stop making your hooks like this"` is title text.

**Absorption rate is the real KPI.** If you say eight words, does the viewer understand all
eight with only one possible meaning? That's 100% absorption. Cut fluff aggressively — but
past a point, cutting words *lowers* absorption because you removed the word that made it
clear. Clarity outranks brevity. Use fewest words, active voice, sixth-grade reading level.

## 2. The 3-step spoken hook (his core formula)

> *"You are driving down the highway at 70mph. I need you to see something, stop, turn around,
> and come back the other way."*

**Step 1 — CONTEXT LEAN.** Two jobs at once: state the topic so plainly the viewer can opt in
*or out* (you don't want the wrong viewer), and get them leaning in. Four ways to make someone
lean: establish common ground · name a pain point or benefit · use a metaphor to simplify ·
tell them something that blows their mind.

**Step 2 — SCROLL-STOP INTERJECTION.** One line, functioning as a stun gun: *"start leaning
in, then immediately you can't move forward, like you ran into a window."* Built with a
contrast word — **but**, however, yet, although. This is the **setup**, not the payoff: "in
order for them to accept the haymaker, you need to stun them first."

**Step 3 — CONTRARIAN SNAPBACK.** A sentence going the *opposite* direction of the lean, still
on topic. The Uno reverse. Bigger shock = bigger snap = better effect.

**Worked example — the Sphere video (8M views):**
> "The tech in the Vegas Sphere is insane. Biggest screen ever built, 20× bigger than an IMAX."
> *[context lean]*
> "**But** get this — the screen is actually the least impressive part of the whole thing."
> *[scroll-stop interjection]*
> "Because the most impressive part is the audio." *[contrarian snapback]*

**Generalised shape:** `If you want X, don't do Y — do Z.`
⚠️ **Critical caveat: Z must actually be compelling.** Run the formula with nothing at the end
of the rainbow and "you're a fraud" — people churn and don't come back.

---

## 3. Contrast — direct beats implied, and the maths of why

All contrast is the distance between what they expect and what you show them.

- **Direct contrast** — you state the common belief out loud, *then* flip it. "Most people
  think X, but actually…"
- **Implied contrast** — you just make the contrarian statement and let them reconstruct the
  common belief themselves. Costs cognitive work.

**Direct wins, and here's the arithmetic he gives:** on a −100 to +100 scale, implied contrast
starts the viewer at 0 and drops them to −100 = a swing of 100. Direct contrast leans them to
+100 *first*, then flips to −100 = a swing of **200**. Stating the common belief also supplies
your context for free.

## 4. The six power words — build a hook without a formula

> *"You don't need a fancy formula, you don't even need to be that good of a writer. All you
> need are six power words."* Four core, two optional.

Running example: *"If I had to grow from 0 to 100K subs on YouTube again in 5 months, here's how I'd do it."*

| # | Word | Job | In the example |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Subject clarity** (core) | Who/what the video is about — I, we, you, or a proper noun | "I" |
| 2 | **Action** (core) | The verb the subject took | "grow" |
| 3 | **Objective / state change** (core) | The end result. Make it as shocking and non-obvious as possible | "0 to 100K subs" |
| 4 | **Contrast** (core) | Base state vs end state, to intensify the A→B curiosity | "0 to" |
| 5 | **Proof** (optional) | Why they should trust your take. Rarely needed for entertainment, often for education | "**again**" |
| 6 | **Time** (optional) | Urgency / speed-to-effectiveness | "in 5 months" |

Note how one phrase often serves as both objective *and* contrast — that's tight writing, not
a mistake. Proof and time are optional in line one because you can embed them in lines two and
three.

## 5. The six hook archetypes (his published mad-libs)

Exact template strings from content.game/p/hooks:

| Archetype | Template | Use when |
|---|---|---|
| **Fortuneteller** | `This [x new thing] is gonna completely change the way [y people] do [z task]. Here's why...` | Industry shifts, product launches, trend-calling |
| **Experimenter** | `I did [x thing] to accomplish [y result] in [z area]… let me show you how I did it` | You actually ran the test. Peer-to-peer |
| **Teacher** | `[X person] was able to achieve [Y result] using [Z unusual method]… let me show you` | Teardowns of *other people's* playbooks |
| **Magician** | `Look at [X thing] right there… that is a [Y explanation]` | Visual stun gun. Needs real motion in frame |
| **Investigator** | `I found a secret feature in [X thing] that will change the way you do [Y task]` | Genuinely lesser-known findings |
| **Contrarian** | `Everyone thinks [X brand] won because of [Y thing], but the truth is, that has nothing to do with it` | You have a real opinion plus evidence |

**Stacking is normal and recommended** — open with the Magician as a visual stun gun, then
transition into one of the other five underneath it.

**The Magician's three routes.** Its two primary routes are in the table above — (i) a **visual
stun tactic** (a snap, a click, an object slammed into frame) and (ii) **the spoken words
themselves** forcing attention ("look at that"). The third is less obvious:

**Route (iii) — the visual pacifier:** a passive activity the subject performs
that holds just enough subconscious attention to keep the viewer locked on the words (doing
makeup, stacking cups, the split-screen gameplay format). Kallaway's own signature: he opens
with *"check this out really quick"* on ~30 consecutive videos — an audible pattern viewers
recognise.

## 6. The nine spoken hook formats (pick one when stuck)

A coarser, more practical taxonomy than the six archetypes — these are the shapes the *spoken*
first line takes. `script.md`'s story-structure table pairs structures with these by name.

| Format | What it does | Best when |
|---|---|---|
| **Secret Reveal** | Teases that you know something they don't | You have an unknown insight or a future implication — news, product releases, freshly-uncovered tactics |
| **Case Study** | "How [subject] achieved [result] via [unexpected method]" | You're breaking down someone else's win. The workhorse for educational creators |
| **Comparison** | Puts two versions side by side — optimal vs suboptimal | A-vs-B content; instant contrast between the desired state and the current one |
| **Question** | Asks the curiosity question outright rather than implying it | Challenge videos, or a question your audience genuinely asks |
| **Education** | Introduces a step-by-step process for one task | Precursor to a tutorial or playbook |
| **List** | Introduces an ordered set of items | Rapid-fire listicles where each item stands alone |
| **Contrarian** | A bold against-the-grain take in sentence one | You have a real opinion, and especially when it'll drive comment debate |
| **Personal Experience** | Opens a first-person story | Narrative videos told from your POV |
| **Problem** | Names a specific pain to set up the solution | You can agitate a pain the viewer already feels |

**How to choose:** don't guess — every niche has one or two formats that always crush, because
certain topics suit certain hook psychology. Study the top performers in *your* niche, find
which format repeats, remix it. This is where `kiwuuu-analyze` earns its keep.

## 7. The four hook commandments — screen every hook against these

1. **Alignment** — visual, text, and spoken all point at the same *idea*, in different *words*
   (§1).
2. **Speed to value** — zero delay, zero fluff. *Test:* stop the video after one sentence — is
   it clear what's coming next?
3. **Clarity** — from that first sentence alone, do they understand the words and the topic?
4. **Curiosity** — read the hook back on its own. Does it make you want to keep reading?

---

## 8. The four hook mistakes — diagnose a dead hook in this order

Each stage assumes the previous is fixed.

**1. DELAY** *(easiest to fix)* — the topic arrives too late. Write the script as numbered
lines; if line 3 is the first mention of the topic, cut lines 1–2 and promote line 3. Retention
is an exponential decay curve, steepest in the first two seconds.
> Bad: *"Guys, this is one of the craziest things I've ever seen, you're never going to believe it."*
> Good: *"Here are three simple ways to improve your gut health."*

**2. CONFUSION** — words or phrasing unclear, so they only absorb half. Fix by writing better:
fewer words (but not fewer than clarity needs), simpler words, active voice.
> **The isolation test:** read *only* the hook, nothing else. Is there more than one way these
> sentences could be interpreted? If yes, rewrite until only your intended reading survives.

**3. IRRELEVANCE** — they understand it but don't believe it's for them. Two fixes:
- **Frame the pain as "you", not "I".** *"I've struggled with skin problems my whole life"*
  invites the viewer to wonder whether they're like you. *"If you've struggled with skin
  problems…"* removes the question entirely.
  ⚠️ **This governs the pain statement only.** First person is fine — and often required — as
  *proof*: *"I tested this on 287 businesses"*, and two of the six archetypes in §5
  (Experimenter, Investigator) are first-person by design. **Pain theirs, proof yours.**
  → SKILL.md "Reconciling the rules" §2.
- **Frame around a pain point they already have.** Need-to-have beats nice-to-have:
  *"These are three common trends in skincare"* → *"If you struggle with acne, try these three things."*

**4. DISINTEREST** — the curiosity problem. No loop opened. Fix with contrast (section 3).

## 9. Visual hooks — 10× more powerful than spoken

Eyes beat ears; speed of light beats speed of sound. Non-negotiables:

- **Something must move in frame in the first second.** High motion, high colour, high
  contrast — contrast against *your category*, not just within the frame (he runs light
  thumbnails where competitors run dark).
- **Put 3–5 words of title text on screen in big bold type. Every video.**
- **A distinctive set/visual identity.** Generic backgrounds no longer stop scrolls — the
  wooden-slat-and-plant podcast set is now invisible.
- Named visual-hook families from his series: **Pattern Interrupts** (ep 1), **Clip Stitch**
  (ep 2 — open on someone else's already-proven viral hook, then react to or transition from
  it), **Framebreaker** (a real-estate example that drove 8M views).

## 10. Sourcing hooks without guessing

His actual loop, which maps 1:1 onto `kiwuuu-analyze`:

1. Build a watchlist of the top accounts in the niche.
2. Sort their videos by **outlier score** (how far a video beat that creator's own average) —
   not by raw views. Outlier score removes channel size from the comparison.
3. Open the top 20–30 and note which of the nine formats each used.
4. **Copy work:** paste the literal winning hook into your doc and swap the words mad-lib
   style. Never ship the copy — ship the structure.
5. **Build a power-word bank.** Cluster the highest-performing words and phrases, then
   generate 5–10 derivatives of each: `scientifically impossible to skip` →
   `psychologically impossible to ignore` → `neurochemically impossible to skip`.

> He reuses the same power phrases across many videos. Power words are reusable assets. Ours
> should live in this file as we find them.
