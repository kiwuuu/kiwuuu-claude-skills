# Ideas — what to make, and whether it can travel

Most content fails before a single word is written, because the idea was guessed. This file is
about not guessing.

---

## 1. The Virality Formula — six variables

> `UNIQUE IDEA + LARGE APPLICABLE AUDIENCE + UNIQUE POINT OF VIEW + WORLD-CLASS HOOK + COMPELLING STORY + LUCK`

Every variable exists to raise one number: **share rate**.

**1. UNIQUE IDEA.** Only two routes:
- *First to market with a common idea* — weak, because everyone reacts to news fast, and the
  algorithm takes a couple of hours to heat up. By then your head start is gone.
- *An uncommon idea* — **the default recommendation.** To find uncommon ideas you have to look
  in uncommon places: weird blogs, small YouTube channels, niche newsletters.
  > "Once you find a weird tree, that tree tends to bear fruit often."

**2. LARGE APPLICABLE AUDIENCE (TAM).** Uncommon ideas usually have small TAM — and sharing a
too-obscure idea is *socially risky* for the sharer. A video on the history of licorice: some
say "that's cool", most think "this guy's a weirdo."

**3. UNIQUE POINT OF VIEW (the story lens).** The lens through which you tell it. A unique POV
makes a *common* idea behave like an uncommon one.

### ⚠️ The combination matrix — the counter-intuitive part

| Idea | Point of view | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Common | Unique POV | ✅ **Sweet spot** |
| Uncommon | Normal POV | ✅ **Sweet spot** |
| Uncommon | Unique POV | ❌ **Fails — too niche to move** |
| Common | Normal POV | ❌ Invisible |

> "You'd think uncommon + unique POV would have a multiplier effect, but it's too niche and
> there aren't enough people who'd react."
> It's easier to go viral with *Taylor Swift + her business strategy* than with *Taylor's dad +
> his business strategy*.

**Worked example (his biggest, 17.5M IG / 11M TikTok):** Messi signs with Inter Miami. Common
idea, enormous audience — but the lens was **the business impact of the deal**, at the
intersection of culture and business. Reporting the news would have been the normal POV, and it
wouldn't have cut through.

**4. WORLD-CLASS HOOK.** Nail the first three variables and you get a solid performer. Add a
world-class hook and you get the outlier. Three reasons it's the most important variable:
- **Share mechanics:** when someone reposts to an Instagram story, **only the first 10 seconds
  play.** Those seconds must re-hook a cold viewer who was sent it.
- **View-curve maths:** exponential decay — the most eyes are at the start.
- **Algorithm:** longer average view duration is what triggers the boost.
> Effort allocation: **~80% of your effort on the first 5 seconds.**

**5. COMPELLING STORY.** Counter-intuitively weighted: nail the first four and "you almost
don't even need that good of a story." A great story only becomes load-bearing when your
audience is small — then people share purely because the story is that good. He calls that an
outlier strategy, not something to optimise for.

**6. LUCK.** One reshare from an account over 100K followers can be the difference between
10K and 1M views. The actionable part: when you see something breaking out with a high share
rate, **DM friends with big accounts and ask them to share it.** Same video posted seven times
on seven days — only one or two go properly viral.

## 2. The seven Idea Legos

A video idea is **not** the topic. The topic is one seventh of it.

1. **Topic** — one sentence on what it's about
2. **Angle** — premise + take + the interesting key facts
3. **Hook structure** — spoken + text + visual
4. **Story structure** — breakdown, listicle, tutorial, narrative…
5. **Visual format** — the layout: green screen, POV vlog, faceless text-conversation, split
   screen
6. **Key visuals** — the actual A-roll, B-roll, animations inside that layout
7. **Audio** — music and sound effects

> **Why most content fails:** you guessed on all seven at once — unvalidated topic, random
> take, unvalidated story and hook structure, a visual format you'd never seen work.

## 3. The three-step idea mining system

**STEP 1 — Outlier research.** Find videos in your niche that already performed, measured by
**outlier score** (how far a video beat *that creator's own average*), not raw views. Outlier
score removes channel size from the comparison — a 40K-view video on a 5K channel is a stronger
signal than a 200K-view video on a 2M channel.

**STEP 2 — Analyse across the seven bricks.** For each outlier, identify all seven, then hunt
for **patterns across the winners**. Six of the top ten cover the same topic? The same visual
format recurs across the top fifty? That's the data telling you the proven answer for that
brick in your niche.

**STEP 3 — Hold the winners constant, remix the rest.** Five-card poker: take a reference
outlier, break it into seven cards, keep the good ones, reshuffle the rest.

⚠️ **The winning pattern per brick changes over time AND is completely different niche to
niche.** Fitness ≠ makeup ≠ marketing. There is no one-size-fits-all, which is exactly why this
is the most valuable content skill to build.

**→ This is what `kiwuuu-analyze` is for.** Run it on outliers, decompose into the seven bricks,
log the recurring answers per channel.

## 4. Where to look — the 2×2 outlier sourcing matrix

Two axes: **content type** (same/different) × **platform** (same/different).

| | Same platform | Different platform |
|---|---|---|
| **Same content type** | ✅ golden goose | ✅ golden goose |
| **Different content type** | ❌ | ❌ |

**Hold the TYPE constant.** Vertical video hits people differently than a blog or a tweet; the
consumption psychology doesn't transfer. From a tweet you can borrow a topic and maybe an
angle, but hook, story structure and visual format don't carry over — so you're back to
guessing on four of the seven bricks.

> The business goal is to **derisk every rep.**

## 5. The 6×5 method — 30 days of content in 30 minutes

**Step 1 — pick 6 subtopics.** Either a 10-minute brain dump of where your ideal customer has
the most questions and pain, or pull the top-performing themes from your channel and
competitors. His examples: *dentistry* → brushing, flossing, cavities, veneers, mouth guards,
gums. *Marketing* → Instagram, YouTube, Facebook ads, email, landing page funnels, AEO.

**Step 2 — pick 5 formats.** Beginners: pick the ones that look easiest to make. Advanced: pick
the formats your competitors have already succeeded with.

**Step 3 — build the grid.** 6 topics × 5 formats = **30 boxes = 30 posting days.** Every day's
assignment is pre-decided, which kills decision fatigue.

*(A 5-3-2 variant appears in a later video — same mechanic, smaller grid.)*

## 6. Topic width — the rule people break

**You always stay inside ONE topic.** "Wider" never means a new topic — it means *subtopics
within the same topic*. Marketing → paid marketing, viral marketing, email marketing. Never
marketing → the NBA.

- Playing the **media game** (chasing views): go wider inside the slice, pick the most
  sensational subtopics.
- Playing for an **offer**: stay narrow, close to the thing you sell.

Getting this wrong is lose-lose: too narrow for views, too wide to convert.

> "The best strategy is pick one topic and hammer that topic until retirement."

## 7. ⚠️ The Views Trap — the failure sequence, in his own words

He says this happened to him. Read it as a warning, since Kiwuuu sits exactly where it bites.

1. Start making content, **get addicted to the views game**.
2. To grow views, broaden the aperture, expand the TAM, become less focused on one avatar.
3. Hit the ceiling — decent money, but capped and decaying as competition and AI content rise.
4. Decide to build an offer to sell to the audience.
5. Discover either that the audience is **all over the place** (because breadth was required for
   views), or that when you push the offer, **not enough of them want it**.
6. Terminal fork: keep playing the media game forever, **or rebuild a second, narrow audience
   from scratch.**

**The sponsorship corollary:** brands pay because they believe your audience matches their
buyer. The wider your content, the less confident that bet, the less they pay.
> "Going wider is antithetical to dollars. Not in a way — that *is* the truth."

## 8. The Five Content Games — pick yours before anything else

Your content strategy is determined by your **business model**, not by taste.

| # | Game | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Entertainment media** | MrBeast, streamers. Max volume, max audience |
| 2 | **Education media** | Videos you can watch and *tactically apply* something from |
| 3 | **Commoditised consumer product** | No education needed — clothing, couches. Pure awareness |
| 4 | **Consumer product needing education** | AG1: you see a green powder and don't know what it is |
| 5 | **B2B / education-heavy** | Services, coaching, info products, B2B SaaS. Needs a personal brand to accrue trust |

Games 1 and 2 are **the media game** — monetised on CPM (AdSense, creator funds, brand deals),
so they need enormous view volume.

**His verdict, and it matters for us:**
> "In general you'll make way more money playing the products, services, offers game than the
> media game." … "That media game is arbing to zero. AI content will run it over. It's a losing
> game — you will lose to MrBeast, Netflix, and the NFL."

**Kiwuuu is bucket five.** WhatsApp AI agents sold to business owners: education-heavy, B2B,
trust-driven. Optimise for the right buyer watching, not the biggest number.

## 9. Seasons of formats — what works decays

Formats saturate as the market fills the opportunity. His three seasons so far:

1. **Low-effort transactional** (TikTok 2020, Reels 2022) — listicles, tutorials, "5 best tools
   for X." Worked because supply was zero. Now largely AI-replicable.
2. **Case-study breakdowns** — "this person achieved this result this way, let me explain."
3. **Personal narrative-driven visual storytelling** *(current)* — "by far the hottest."

Why season 3 wins: lived experience is one-of-one and therefore hard to copy. He notes the
irony that he helped saturate season 1 by open-sourcing the playbook.

**The narrative beat structure:**
> "I experienced this problem. Here's what I tried to solve it. Didn't work. Here's what I *did*
> do to solve it. If you have a similar problem, here you go."

Less *"you should do these three things"*, more *"I did this thing, take it or leave it."*

**The commoditisation test:** if 50 other people could make your video, it won't cut through
unless you're the best of the 50 or you already have a brand.

## 10. Series — the bingeability engine

Numbering a series is a retention *and* discovery device.

> "The brain is a puzzle machine. If you need 10 pieces and I show you eight, everyone wants
> nine and ten to complete the puzzle. If you post day four and I like it, I'll go back for
> days one, two, three."

It forces **connective tissue** between videos, which converts one view into a session. His own
shorts do this constantly — *"this is best visual hooks episode 2"*, *"best viral formats
episode 3"* — and he says the series name in line one.

**Series types that outperform:**

| Type | Shape | Example for us |
|---|---|---|
| **Docuseries** | Follows one unfolding real thing across episodes | Building a client's agent, start to live |
| **Numbered Countdown** | "N of 10" — the puzzle-completion driver | "10 ways businesses lose money on WhatsApp" |
| **Progress Journey** | A measurable number moving over time | "$0 to first paying customer, week N" |
| **Recurring Segment** | Same format, new subject each time | "Teardown Tuesday: this company's reply flow" |
| **Skit Universe** | Recurring characters in a fictional world | The Absurdity/REKT character channels |

*(His post names six categories but enumerates only these five — the sixth isn't published.)*

⚠️ **The guardrail:** if you commit to a daily numbered series and stop, you break the promise
you set. **Stack episodes before you launch.** Don't start on the treadmill.

## 11. Positioning — the five differentiation paths

Be top 3–5 in your niche in exactly **one** of these:

1. **Visual packaging** — editing, world-building
2. **Originality** — non-obvious ideas
3. **Tactical usefulness** — immediately applicable
4. **Rare** — a unique skill/experience combination
5. **Aura** — exceptional likability

His maths: `3–5 people per category × 5 categories ≈ 15–25 top people per niche`. Pick the lane
you can actually win.

**The three-layer model:** topic consistency is the cake, your differentiation factor is the
icing, personality is the sprinkles. *You are not the niche* — make content about what the
viewer is interested in, not what you're interested in.

**Expertise-vs-personality mix by business model** (his prescribed ratios):

| Business model | Expertise / Personality |
|---|---|
| DFY services agency | 75 / 25 |
| Software / B2B SaaS | **80 / 20** |
| Info products / coaching | 65 / 35 |
| Media — deep education | 50 / 50 |
| Media — entertainment | 20 / 80 |
| Consumer products | 10 / 90 |

**Kiwuuu sits at ~75–80 / 20–25** — a B2B SaaS-and-services blend. Expertise opens the door;
personality is the tiebreaker in a crowded category.
